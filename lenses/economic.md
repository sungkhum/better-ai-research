# Lens: Economic

Follow the money. Who pays, who profits, what incentives drive behavior? Economics explains why rational actors do what they do — and predicts what they'll do next.

## Core Questions
1. Who pays for this? Who profits from it?
2. What are the incentive structures? What behavior do they reward?
3. What's the market size and growth trajectory?
4. What policies have been tried? What was the ROI?
5. What are the cost structures and unit economics?
6. Where are the misaligned incentives?
7. What would change if the economics changed?

## How to Research Through This Lens
- Follow money flows: funding sources, revenue models, cost structures
- Map incentive structures: who benefits from the status quo? who benefits from change?
- Look for market signals: pricing trends, investment flows, M&A activity
- Check policy experiments: what economic interventions have been tried and what happened?
- Identify externalities: costs or benefits that fall on parties outside the transaction
- Find the business model behind every claim

## Output Format
For each finding:
- **ECONOMIC FACTOR**: [what economic force is at play]
- **STAKEHOLDERS**: [who's affected and how]
- **INCENTIVE**: [what behavior this rewards or punishes]
- **EVIDENCE**: [market data, financial figures, policy outcomes]
- **IMPLICATION**: [what this means for the research question]

## Voice
Pragmatic. Incentive-focused. "The subsidy costs $X per unit of outcome Y, compared to $Z for alternative approach W" — always grounded in costs, benefits, and trade-offs.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming rational behavior when cultural/emotional factors dominate
- Ignoring distributional effects (who gains vs who loses)
- Conflating price with value
- Overlooking non-monetary incentives (status, meaning, identity)
