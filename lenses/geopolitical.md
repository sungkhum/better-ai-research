# Lens: Geopolitical

Zoom out to the global chessboard. Which countries, which power dynamics, which alliances and conflicts shape this? Almost nothing important happens in isolation.

## Core Questions
1. Which countries or regions are most affected?
2. What power shifts does this create or accelerate?
3. What alliances, treaties, or institutions are relevant?
4. How do different jurisdictions regulate or approach this?
5. What are the national security implications?
6. Who has leverage, and how might they use it?
7. What are the second-order geopolitical effects?

## How to Research Through This Lens
- Map the key state and institutional actors
- Identify resource dependencies and supply chains
- Check regulatory environments across major jurisdictions
- Look for strategic competition: US-China, EU-US, regional rivalries
- Find where economic interests conflict with political alliances
- Assess soft power dynamics: cultural influence, technological standards
- Consider domestic politics: what leaders need to do to stay in power

## Output Format
For each finding:
- **ACTOR**: [country, institution, or bloc]
- **INTEREST**: [what they want and why]
- **LEVERAGE**: [what power they have in this domain]
- **ACTION**: [what they're doing or likely to do]
- **IMPLICATION**: [how this shapes the research question]

## Voice
Strategic. Power-aware. "China controls 80% of rare earth processing, giving it significant leverage over..." — always grounded in who has power, who wants it, and what they'll do with it.

## Common Pitfalls
- Viewing everything through one country's lens (usually US-centric)
- Ignoring non-state actors (corporations, NGOs, movements)
- Assuming rational state behavior when domestic politics drive decisions
- Missing the role of institutions (WTO, UN, IMF) in constraining action
- Conflating what leaders say with what they do
