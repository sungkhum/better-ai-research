# Contradiction Protocol

What to do when sources or lenses disagree. This is where the real insights hide — contradictions are features, not bugs.

## When Two Sources Disagree

### Step 1: Check the Basics
- Are they talking about the same thing? (different geographies, time periods, definitions)
- Are they using the same data? (same dataset, different interpretation?)
- Is one source a higher tier than the other? (see [[source-evaluation]])

### Step 2: Find the Root of Disagreement
Usually one of:
- **Different data**: they're looking at different datasets → find out which is more complete/recent
- **Different interpretation**: same data, different conclusions → examine their reasoning chain
- **Different scope**: one is global, one is country-specific → both might be right in their context
- **Different timeframe**: short-term vs long-term trends can look opposite
- **Different incentives**: one has reason to spin the data → check funding, affiliations
- **Different definitions**: they're measuring different things and calling it the same name

### Step 3: Document, Don't Resolve
In the synthesis, write it as:

> "Source A argues [X] based on [data]. Source B argues [Y] based on [data]. The disagreement likely stems from [root cause]. Under conditions [C1], A is probably right. Under conditions [C2], B is probably right."

### Step 4: Upgrade to Open Questions if Unresolvable
If after honest effort you can't determine who's right:
- This IS a finding. "We don't know whether X or Y" is valuable intelligence
- Add it to the project's open-questions.md with the specific evidence needed to resolve it
- This often becomes the most interesting part of the research
- It may become the starting question for a future research project

## When Two Lenses Disagree

This is expected and healthy. Different lenses SHOULD produce tension.

Common productive tensions:
- [[technical]] vs [[economic]]: "The tech works but the economics don't" (or vice versa)
- [[historical]] vs [[first-principles]]: "History says X, but conditions have fundamentally changed"
- [[contrarian]] vs everything else: The whole point — stress-testing consensus
- [[geopolitical]] vs [[economic]]: "It makes economic sense but political incentives prevent it"

When lenses conflict:
1. State both positions clearly with evidence
2. Identify what would have to be true for each lens to be correct
3. Ask: is there a framing where BOTH are right in different contexts?
4. The tension itself often reveals the most important insight

## Confidence Adjustment

- 2 lenses agree, 1 disagrees → investigate the disagreeing lens deeper. it might see something the others miss
- 3-4 lenses agree, 1-2 disagree → moderate-to-high confidence, but document the dissent
- All 6 lenses agree → **be suspicious**. you might have confirmation bias. re-read [[contrarian]] more carefully
- No lenses agree → your research question might be too broad. narrow the scope

## The "Steel Man" Test

Before dismissing any contradicting source or lens:
1. State the opposing view in its STRONGEST possible form
2. Identify what evidence would make it correct
3. Check if that evidence exists
4. Only then assess whether the contradiction is real or just a different vantage point
